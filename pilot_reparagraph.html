Re-paragraph pilot — ten samples

Smith's ask (DECISIONS, "explanations too paragraphy"): re-paragraph / light bullets to make good long explanations digestible. Ten longest single-block explanations, one per topic. Wording is byte-identical in every "Re-paragraphed" column — only paragraph breaks inserted at the house style's beat boundaries. tc_imp_pp_con_05tense_choice
Today — one block
'Conoscevo già la sua musica perché avevo letto un articolo su una rivista qualche mese prima.' means 'I already knew her music because I'd read an article in a magazine a few months earlier.' When you say you 'already knew' a body of work coming into a moment, you're describing a state of prior familiarity. Italian's modal verb conoscere uses the imperfect indicative for that standing-familiarity reading, even when the object is a creative output rather than a person. The cues 'già' and the explanatory 'perché avevo letto un articolo...qualche mese prima' anchor the familiarity in time. Ho conosciuto la sua musica would point to a moment of first encounter, which doesn't fit a sentence that's locating the familiarity earlier than the current scene.

tpp_av_dop_le_01verb_form.trapassato_prossimo
Today — one block
'Cercavo le chiavi dappertutto: le avevo messe in tasca poco prima!' means 'I was hunting everywhere for the keys: I'd put them in my pocket a little earlier!' The keys had been put in the pocket before the searching, so the trapassato prossimo: the imperfect of avere (avevo) plus the participle. There is also a preceding direct-object pronoun le (standing for le chiavi), and with a preceding direct-object pronoun the participle agrees, a rule carried over from the passato prossimo. Le chiavi are feminine plural, so the participle of mettere becomes messe, giving le avevo messe. The present auxiliary (ho) would make it a passato prossimo, and messo or messa would miss the agreement.

imp_st_narr_02verb_form.imperfect
Today — one block
'The accident happened at ten in the evening. The police arrived shortly after, and the rescue services came to the scene': L'incidente succedeva alle dieci di sera. La polizia arrivava poco dopo, e i soccorsi venivano sul posto. Newspaper reporting often uses the narrative imperfect to recount sequences of past events vividly. The chain 'succedeva... arrivava... venivano' creates a stretched-out, scene-by-scene effect that the PP or passato remoto wouldn't carry. The other two verbs (arrivava, venivano) are given in the narrative imperfect; this item tests the matching style choice for succedere.

prem_are_parlare_3sg_02verb_form.passato_remoto
Today — one block
'Quella sera Marco parlò a lungo davanti a tutti.' (That evening Marco spoke at length in front of everyone.) The lui/lei form of a regular -are passato remoto ends in a stressed -ò, and the written accent is obligatory: parlare gives parlò. The accent is not decoration. It marks where the stress falls (par-LÒ, not PAR-lo) and it is the only thing on the page that separates this remote past from parlo, the io present of the same verb. Marco could not take parlo anyway (his present would be parla), so the accent here is carrying the tense, not resolving a clash of persons.

ger_gerpass_finire_avere_01verb_form.gerundio
Today — one block
'Avendo finito i compiti, è uscito a giocare.' means 'Having finished his homework, he went out to play.'. The compound gerund (gerundio passato) marks an action finished before the main one: 'having finished his homework, he went out'. It is built like the passato prossimo, avere or essere plus the past participle, but with the auxiliary in its gerund form. Finire takes avere here (it has a direct object, i compiti), so the auxiliary is avendo, and the participle is finito: avendo finito. With avere the participle does not agree with the subject.

rep_futcond_01reported_speech
Today — one block
'Marco ha detto che sarebbe venuto alla festa' means 'Marco said he would come to the party.' When the reporting verb is in the past, a quoted future slides back. English slides it to 'would come', and that is exactly the trap: Italian goes one step further, to the past conditional 'sarebbe venuto'. The present conditional 'verrebbe' is the direct calque of the English and the classic miss. In speech you will also hear 'veniva', which is accepted here at half credit but is not the standard written form.

fu_prob_ant_01verb_form.future
Today — one block
'He must have missed the train': Non è ancora arrivato: avrà perso il treno. Just as the simple future guesses about the present, the futuro anteriore guesses about the PAST: 'avrà perso il treno' means 'he's probably missed the train'. The plain passato prossimo 'ha perso' would state it as known; the compound future carries the supposition about a completed action. Two skills sit here: choosing the future-of-probability for a past guess, and building the futuro anteriore (avrà + perso).

cond_are_parlare_1sg_01verb_form.condizionale
Today — one block
"Al posto tuo, parlerei con il direttore." (In your place, I would speak with the director.) When you say what you would do in someone else's place, you reach for the conditional. A regular -are verb builds it on the very stem you already use for the future (parlare gives parler-, shifting the theme vowel a to e), then adds the conditional ending -ei for io: parlerei. Keep the a (parlarei) and you have made the commonest -are slip; the stem is the same as parlerò, only the ending changes.

adj_pos_pre_01adjective_agreement
Today — one block
'A little house': una piccola casa Both orderings are grammatical. Piccolo belongs to a small set — bello, buono, grande, nuovo, vecchio, giovane, bravo, lungo, breve — that sit before the noun by default, in canonical pre-noun position. Una piccola casa is the unmarked everyday order, no special emphasis. Una casa piccola is also grammatical but marked, specifically emphasising size ('small as opposed to large'). For the default reading, use the pre-noun order: una piccola casa.

rel_cui_prep_01relative_pronoun
Today — one block
'Il film di cui parlavo è ancora al cinema' means 'The film I was talking about is still on at the cinema.' Parlare takes di ('parlare di qualcosa'), and when a relative pronoun carries a preposition, che steps aside for cui, with the preposition sitting in front: di cui. English lets the preposition dangle at the end ('the film I was talking about'); Italian moves it up front and never strands it. The agreeing alternative del quale is also correct and a touch more formal.
